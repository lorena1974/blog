Is Bitcoin Mining on the Decline?
I was asked why I believed Bitcoin mining companies were shifting toward the Artificial Intelligence industry.
The crypto ecosystem is seeing a shift not only among Bitcoin miners but also among projects, institutional capital, and investment funds toward companies and stocks related to Artificial Intelligence.
First, there is a significant outflow of investment from Bitcoin ETFs and crypto companies toward other sectors. In May, nearly $3 billion flowed out of Bitcoin ETFs (K33 Research Report).
Second, several AI companies are expected to go public. This is known as an IPO, or initial public offering, which is the process by which a private company sells its shares to the public for the first time. Several IPOs are currently in the works. In fact, on June 1, Anthropic (Claude AI) filed confidential documentation with the SEC to go public. Anthropic’s valuation is estimated at $965 billion following the completion of a $65 billion funding round.
SpaceX has an estimated valuation of $1.75 trillion, is scheduled to go public on June 12, and aims to raise $75 billion (it is the most anticipated of all).
OpenAI raised $122 billion in a funding round, and it is estimated—though unconfirmed—that it will move forward with an IPO in the coming weeks.
Now, regarding the reason for the migration of bitcoin miners to the artificial intelligence industry, it must be understood that, just like bitcoin mining, AI requires high energy consumption for training AI models. Hardware resources are scarce, and there is competition between bitcoin mining and AI.
In turn, there are many other reasons, such as:
1.- Bitcoin mining is becoming centralized,
2.- Profitability and earnings from bitcoin mining have been significantly reduced due to the post-halving period,
3.- The profits generated by AI training data centers are more stable and predictable due to the long-term contracts signed with AI companies and do not depend on the price of bitcoin,
4.- Companies are seeking to diversify their operations,
5.- Bitcoin mining infrastructure already has access to the power grid, and cooling systems can serve as AI data centers,
6.- The institutional market finds attractive opportunities in AI-related companies, which boosts the stock prices of publicly traded bitcoin mining firms.
These reasons, which are not exhaustive, provide a perfect framework for understanding why bitcoin mining companies are joining forces with tech firms to train AI models.
For reference, among the leading bitcoin mining companies spearheading the transition to AI are: MARA (NASDAQ: MARA), Riot Platforms (NASDAQ: RIOT), IREN (NASDAQ: IREN), and Hut 8 Corp. (NASDAQ: HUT). Several others are also on the list: CleanSpark (NASDAQ: CLSK), Core Scientific (NASDAQ: CORZ), Cipher Mining (NASDAQ: CIFR), TeraWulf (NASDAQ: WULF), Bitfarms (NASDAQ: BITF), and Bitdeer Technologies Group (NASDAQ: BTDR).
Companies have begun to allocate their mining capacity to artificial intelligence data centers to increase their profits and attract more investment.
This is just the beginning. We’ll see how it turns out.
